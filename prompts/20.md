## 📝 Prompt

```
She starts with Tai Chi.

Thirty seconds later, the ocean becomes a dragon.

I gave Seedance 2.5 no timestamps and no second-by-second shot list—only a story arc: stillness, rising force, a water-dragon climax, and a quiet ending.

One 30-second generation. 780 credits.

It understood the buildup far better than I expected.

Here is the prompt I used:

Create a 30-second, 9:16 vertical live-action Eastern fantasy short film.

The input reference image shows multiple facial views of the same young adult Asian woman. Use it only to preserve her facial features, skin tone, shoulder-length black hair, pearl stud earrings, age, and identity. Do not inherit the clothing shown in the reference image. Only this one protagonist may appear throughout the final video. Do not show a collage, split screen, studio backdrop, or duplicated versions of the character.

She wears a lightweight modern Tai Chi-inspired wuxia outfit in white and pale sage, with long sleeves and layered fabric that move naturally with the wind and her body. She stands barefoot on wet sand. Her face, hairstyle, age, clothing, and body proportions must remain consistent throughout the entire video.

The story takes place on a beach shortly before a storm. The sky hangs low, the sea wind moves her clothing, and the distant waves gradually become more turbulent.

Open with restraint and stillness. Establish the setting with a wide view of the coastline. She stands facing the sea for a moment, then slowly raises her hands and begins a grounded, fluid, realistic Tai Chi sequence. Her wrists, fingers, shoulders, steps, balance, and breathing should follow believable human movement. It should not look like dance, and she should not perform exaggerated jumps.

As she moves through cloud hands and turns her body, ripples appear in the shallow water around her feet. The sea responds only subtly at first. Then several transparent ribbons of water begin to form and follow the direction of her arms. Every movement of the water must be visibly caused by her Tai Chi gestures.

Her movements gradually become more powerful. The camera slowly moves from a medium view toward her side as the water ribbons rise and gather behind her. Do not reveal the final form too early. Let the force and the water accumulate step by step.

At the climax, she completes a full turn, settles her balance, and pushes both palms forward. The gathered seawater rises violently and forms an enormous transparent Eastern dragon made from realistic water. The dragon circles her once, then surges toward the approaching black storm clouds.

The water dragon breaks through the clouds. The darkness tears open and golden sunlight pours back across the sea. The dragon loses its form and falls back into the ocean as fine rain.

Return to stillness for the ending. She slowly completes the Tai Chi form and lowers her hands. The waves become calm again. Hold on her silhouette facing the golden sea for a moment before the film ends naturally.

The entire video must feel like one continuously developing story, not a collection of unrelated effects shots. Use only a small number of motivated cuts. Do not cut for every individual movement. Give the opening room to breathe, let the power rise gradually, reveal the dragon only at the climax, and fully show the closing movement and the sea returning to calm.

Use only synchronized environmental sound: waves, wind, fabric movement, bare footsteps on wet sand, gathering water, and a low resonant sound from the water dragon. No dialogue, no narration, no background music, no subtitles, no text, no logo, and no watermark.

Avoid identity drift, remnants of the original reference clothing, costume changes, malformed limbs or fingers, sliding feet, water effects without a visible cause, revealing the dragon too early, excessive hard cuts, cartoon water, video-game visual effects, and a climax without buildup.
```
